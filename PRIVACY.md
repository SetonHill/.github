# Privacy Policy

Version: 0.3.0

## Public and Private Repositories

Assume every public repository is visible worldwide and may be copied or indexed. Publish only approved course foundations, templates, safe examples, and explicitly released work. Student and team project repositories are private by default, but private visibility does not make them suitable for unrestricted or regulated data.

## What May Be Committed

Commit source code, documentation, tests, synthetic data, public data, specifically approved de-identified classroom or research data, sanitized configuration examples, and course evidence approved for that repository. Prefer fictional names, generated identifiers, and synthetic datasets. De-identification must remove direct and reasonably linkable identifiers.

## What Must Not Be Committed

Do not commit regulated, confidential, student-record, credential, private-infrastructure, production, or otherwise restricted data unless the university, IRB, and IT approvals required for that repository and system have been obtained. This includes grades, accommodations, rosters, government or institutional identifiers, private contact information, recovery codes, private keys, `.env` files, restricted research data, licensed material, and data about people collected without an approved process.

Screenshots and logs must be reviewed before upload. Remove usernames when they are not required, plus email addresses, tokens, addresses, hostnames, IP addresses, repository invitations, browser tabs, notifications, and other unintended context. Never use redaction that can be reversed from the original file or metadata.

## Identity and Retention

Git history records usernames and contribution activity. Even in private repositories, GitHub usernames, commits, pull requests, reviews, and organization or team membership may reveal identity to authorized participants. Rosters, accommodations, grades, and private team assignments must never be exposed publicly. Public exposure of student work requires a separate explicit approval process.

Access is reviewed at course close. Six months after the course ends, repositories may be archived or deleted according to faculty and IT requirements, subject to legal-hold, research, records, or IT exceptions. Students receive notice and a reasonable export window before deletion.

An approved portfolio export goes only to the student's private repository after faculty review, teammate consent where applicable, licensing review, secret and security scans, removal of student/private/infrastructure data, and compliance with research restrictions. Public portfolio publication requires a separate later approval.

Use the official university privacy or student-records channel. Keep rosters in the approved private course-administration system. Do not invent a reporting URL or identify students publicly.

## Legal Hold and Required Preservation

A valid legal or litigation hold, records-preservation directive, subpoena-related preservation notice, research-integrity hold, or other authorized university preservation directive suspends ordinary deletion, archival disposal, offboarding destruction, history rewriting, transfer or export deletion, and automated cleanup for identified material. A student export does not replace the authoritative held record.

Preserve identified repositories and available related metadata in their existing form. Do not sanitize retroactively, delete, force-push, rewrite history, destroy relevant evidence, or remove relevant accounts and access records. Limit access under least privilege; a hold does not authorize public visibility or disclosure of its existence or scope beyond authorized recipients.

Follow official written preservation instructions and escalate conflicts to the named university legal, records, IT, or research authority. Only the authorized issuing or releasing authority may end the hold in writing. After release, normal retention resumes as documented. Where GitHub retention is insufficient, coordinate with university-authorized archival or export systems. This is operational policy, not legal advice; official university and legal instructions control.
